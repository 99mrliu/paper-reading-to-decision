Scope. Our object of study is first-stage retrieval quality,
measured with standard ranking metrics, plus the recovery
obtained from one reranking stage. We do not evaluate an-
swer generation. Because the tax is largely a ranking-position
effect (recall@10 is preserved in aggregate, although a mi-
nority of the hurt queries do lose a relevant document from
the top-10; Section V-D), its consequence for a downstream
consumer depends on how rank-sensitive that consumer is;
the reranking experiment measures the case that matters most
in practice, and we state the remaining exposure without
claiming an effect on generated answers.
II. RELATED WORK
A. DENSE AND SPARSE RETRIEVAL
Dense retrievers such as DPR [2] and late-interaction models
such as ColBERT [10] match queries and documents in
an embedding space and therefore handle paraphrase and
synonymy, but can miss rare terms, identifiers, and out-
of-distribution vocabulary. BM25 [1] remains a strong and
cheap baseline, especially for keyword- and entity-heavy
queries, and its Anserini/Pyserini implementation [11], [12]
is the reference point for BEIR [13], [14]. Learned sparse
models such as SPLADE [15], [16] add learned term expan-
sion to an inverted-index representation. Because each family
fails on a different slice of queries, hybrid fusion is attractive;
for the same reason, aggregate gains can coexist with per-
query harm.
B. HYBRID FUSION AND THE RRF DEFAULT
Hybrid systems combine sparse and dense rankings either
by rank or by score. RRF [3] sums reciprocal ranks with a
constant k: it is parameter-light and scale-free, but discards
score magnitude, treating a confidently top-ranked document
identically to a marginal one. Score-based methods (Comb-
SUM/CombMNZ [17], [18] and weighted interpolation [19])
preserve magnitude but must first place two incomparable
score distributions on a common scale.

the winning SIGIR 2025 LiveRAG system fuses BM25 and

teams used hybrid search [29], as do RAG-Fusion [30] and
several 2025–2026 systems [31]–[33]. The default is not
universal: OpenSearch’s hybrid pipeline defaults to min–max
normalization with an arithmetic mean and added RRF only
in version 2.19 [34], and Weaviate reversed its default from
rank fusion to min–max “relative score” fusion in version
1.24 after internal benchmarks [35], [36]; Vespa’s tutorial
reports linear normalization beating RRF on NFCorpus [37].
