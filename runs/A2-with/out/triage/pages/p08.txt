D. THE FUSION TAX: HARM AND BENEFIT
Table 8 gives the symmetric accounting of Section III-B for
interpolation and RRF against both references, and Fig. 2 the
distribution of τ.
Oracle tax. Against the per-query oracle, the best method
(interpolation, α = 0.7) hurts 12.8% of queries on the easy,
dense-dominated Quora set, 20–39% on five corpora, and
48% on the 50-query TREC-COVID set; RRF hurts 20–
40% and CombMNZ 17–40% (per-method rates and mean
losses in Supplementary Table S9). These are not near-ties:
requiring a material loss of τ > 0.05 nDCG@10 still leaves
10–34% of queries (interpolation) and τ > 0.10 leaves 8–
22% (Supplementary Table S9), and the mean loss on a
hurt query is 0.09–0.25 nDCG@10. Bootstrap confidence
intervals (Supplementary Table S2) sit well above zero on
every corpus. Fusion beats both of its components on only
4–40% of queries, so the net change against the oracle is
negative everywhere (−0.018 to −0.049): a perfect per-query
router would gain 0.02–0.05 nDCG@10 over static fusion on
every corpus, which is the prize that motivates routing and
that Section V-G shows is hard to collect.
Deployable tax and benefit. Against always-dense, the
reference a practitioner can actually deploy, interpolation
hurts 8–21% of queries and helps 12–72%. The mean gain
on a helped query equals or exceeds the mean loss on a
hurt query on every corpus, by under 5% on SciFact and
ArguAna (0.213 vs. 0.205, 0.221 vs. 0.213) and by 1.2–1.8×
on the others (e.g. FiQA 0.166 vs. 0.100, NFCorpus 0.077
vs. 0.044), so fusion’s aggregate edge, positive on all seven
corpora (+0.008 to +0.051), comes from helping somewhat
more queries, and somewhat more per query, than it hurts.
The losses are nevertheless of the same order as the gains,
0.04–0.21 nDCG@10 on a hurt query, not small change. For
RRF the balance tips: it hurts 15–33% of queries and its
net change against dense-only is negative on five corpora
(−0.002 to −0.039), positive only on the two balanced
corpora (NFCorpus +0.020, TREC-COVID +0.135).
By quadrant. Table 7 decomposes the interpolation tax
by quadrant. In Sparse-Only, dense-heavy fusion hurts 87–
95% of queries with a net loss of 0.14–0.37 nDCG@10: the
dense side contributes only noise there and a weight of 0.7
buries BM25’s relevant documents. In Dense-Only it hurts
38–65% (net −0.03 to −0.11), the mirror image with BM25
as the noise source. In Both-Win the two directions nearly
cancel (net −0.04 to +0.01) while 10–48% of queries are
still reordered for the worse, and in Both-Fail fusion can only
help, which it does on 0–9% of queries. The harm rate is thus
highest exactly where Fig. 1 places it: off the diagonal, on
the at most 29% of queries that only one retriever covers, and
the dense-heavy weight that wins in aggregate does so by
sacrificing the Sparse-Only minority. Rate and count differ,
however: because Both-Win is by far the largest quadrant, it
holds 38–72% of the hurt queries and 26–63% of the total
loss (all of both on TREC-COVID, whose 50 queries are
all Both-Win), so fixing only the off-diagonal queries would
leave most of the tax in place (Supplementary Table S14).
Mostly ranking, not coverage. Fusion’s recall@10 equals
or exceeds dense-only’s in aggregate on all seven corpora
(Supplementary Table S5), and recall is reduced on only 1–
7% of all queries. The hurt queries themselves are less clean:
among the queries interpolation hurts relative to dense-only,
10–43% also lose at least one relevant document from the
top-10, those queries carry 15–59% of the total nDCG@10
downside (15–35% on four corpora, 48–59% on NFCorpus,
SciDocs, and TREC-COVID), and 77 queries across the
seven corpora lose every relevant document from the top-10
(Supplementary Table S5). For most hurt queries the relevant
documents remain in the top-10 and fusion reorders them, so
the harm falls mainly on consumers sensitive to rank order,
but a minority of it is a coverage loss that no downstream
reordering can repair; Section V-H measures how much of
the rest a second stage removes.
Risk-sensitive significance. When the deployable com-
parison is placed on the standard risk-sensitive footing [57],
[58], TRisk (Supplementary Table S3) flags interpolation as
significantly risky relative to always-dense at risk aversion
αrisk = 5 on six of the seven corpora (all but TREC-COVID)
even though it beats dense-only on the mean, and already at
αrisk = 1 on ArguAna; RRF is significantly risky on five
corpora at αrisk = 1 and six at αrisk = 5. TREC-COVID,
where both components are strong, is the one corpus on
which both fusions are risk-robust.
