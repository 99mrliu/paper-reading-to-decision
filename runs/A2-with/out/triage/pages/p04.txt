III. PROBLEM FORMULATION AND TERMINOLOGY
We fix the vocabulary used in the rest of the paper here; each
term is defined once and used verbatim afterwards.
A. DIAGNOSTIC TAXONOMY
For a query q, let NDCGs(q) and NDCGd(q) denote the
nDCG@10 of the sparse and dense retrievers. We classify q
into one of four quadrants by whether each retriever places
at least one relevant document in its top-10:
• Both-Win: NDCGs(q) > 0 and NDCGd(q) > 0;
• Dense-Only: NDCGd(q) > 0 and NDCGs(q) = 0;
• Sparse-Only: NDCGs(q) > 0 and NDCGd(q) = 0;
• Both-Fail: NDCGs(q) = 0 and NDCGd(q) = 0.
The criterion is deliberately coarse: it records which retriever
covers a query, not how well. Magnitudes are carried by the
tax below.

Fusion hurts q when τ(q) > 0 and helps it when τ(q) < 0;
this sign convention (positive is harm) is used in every
table and figure. Because BEIR corpora carry multiple, often
graded, relevant documents per query, a positive tax means
that fusion demoted relevant documents relative to the better
component, which may be the loss of the single relevant
document or a reordering among several; we therefore speak
of relevant documents rather than of a correct answer.
The reference in Eq. 1 is a per-query oracle: it presumes
knowledge of which retriever wins each query, and Sec-
tion V-G shows that neither fusion failure nor the dense-
versus-fusion decision is predictable well enough from cheap
features for a router to exploit. We therefore report two
quantities. The oracle tax is the fraction of queries with
τ(q) > 0, an upper bound on per-query harm. The deploy-
able tax replaces the oracle by the best static single retriever,
always-dense (the stronger component on every corpus with
the primary encoder, BGE-small): it is the fraction of queries
on which fusion scores below dense-only, i.e. the harm a
practitioner who simply chose dense retrieval would avoid.
Both are accompanied by the symmetric benefit statistics
(fraction of queries helped and mean gain when helped) and
by the net change, so that harm is never reported without the
corresponding benefit. For a fusion method f and baseline b
the net mean change decomposes as
E[∆] = P(help) E[gain | help] −P(hurt) E[loss | hurt],

URisk [57]; the risk-sensitive tests in Section V-D weight the
