specific to one cross-encoder (BGE-reranker-v2-m3) at one

about that configuration and should not be read as a general
upper bound on what reranking can recover, since a different
reranker, a deeper candidate list, or a listwise LLM reranker
could recover more of the tax or less of it, and rerankers
themselves degrade with pool size [67].

VI. DISCUSSION
A. RRF IS NOT A SAFE DEFAULT, AND WHEN IT IS THE
RIGHT CHOICE
Our results qualify the default recommendation of RRF
that the documentation surveyed in Section II-B carried at
the time of writing. On the five corpora where the dense
retriever is clearly the stronger component, dense-weighted
interpolation beats RRF in aggregate (significantly on four),
RRF’s per-query tax against the oracle is higher (20–40% vs.
13–36%), and RRF’s net change against simply using the
dense retriever is negative. RRF discards score magnitude,
the information that protects the documents the stronger
component ranked highly (a document ranked first by a
confident retriever counts the same as one ranked first by a
hair), but it also weights the two components equally where
the interpolation it loses to weights dense 0.7. The two effects
can be separated: a weighted RRF with the same 0.7/0.3
weights and no score magnitudes closes 25–73% of the RRF-
to-interpolation gap on the five dense-led corpora (Supple-
mentary Table S13), so weighting accounts for roughly half
of RRF’s disadvantage and the score information for the rest.

interpolation by 0.08–0.09 nDCG@10 and help 70–74% of
