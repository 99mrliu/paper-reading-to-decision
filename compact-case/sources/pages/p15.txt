E. WHY PER-QUERY ROUTING IS HARD
A natural response to the tax is to fuse only when fusion
is predicted to help. Section V-G shows why this is harder
than it looks: the failure signal exists within a corpus but
its carriers and their directions change across corpora, so a
router calibrated on one corpus can be at or below chance
on another, the practitioner has no cheap way to know which,
and the held-out router of Supplementary Table S11 confirms
that acting on the signal does not pay. This is consistent with
QPP-weighted fusion working best when tuned to a target
distribution [49] and with the observation that classical QPP
degrades on neural rankers [48]. It also frames the per-query
gap that routing would have to close: the oracle gains 0.02–
0.05 nDCG@10 over static fusion on every corpus, which is
worth pursuing, but the evidence here says the signal must
come from richer, per-corpus, or model-based sources rather
than transferable heuristics.
F. TEMPORAL STABILITY AS ENCODERS EVOLVE
The ladder spans encoder generations from 2021 (MiniLM)
through 2023 (BGE v1.5) to 2025 (Qwen3-Embedding), and
the 2025 model pays a tax of 14–43%, a range overlap-
ping those of the 2021 and 2023 models, despite being the
strongest encoder in the study on four corpora. Two struc-
tural reasons suggest the tax will outlive the next encoder
generation as well: the off-diagonal quadrants are defined by
disagreement between two retrievers, which a better dense
model reduces only on the dense side, and score interpolation
must still normalize two incomparable distributions, which
no encoder improvement changes. What a newer encoder
does change is the sparse–dense balance, and hence which
fusion method and weight are appropriate, so the balance
should be re-measured whenever the encoder is updated.
G. LIMITATIONS AND FUTURE WORK
(1) We evaluate first-stage retrieval and one reranking stage,
not answer generation; the reranking experiment measures
the exposure of a retrieve-then-rerank pipeline at one depth
with one reranker, and the effect on generated answers re-
mains open. (2) The reranking results are for one cross-
encoder applied to the top-30 candidates of each source; they
are not a general upper bound on reranking recovery, since
deeper reranking or a different reranker could recover more
of the tax, or less, as rerankers themselves degrade with pool
size [67]. (3) TREC-COVID has 50 queries, so its estimates
carry wide intervals, and the multi-million-document BEIR
corpora are omitted under a CPU budget. (4) Efficiency was
measured on one consumer workstation without a discrete
GPU; absolute latencies will differ on production hardware,
and so will the ratios between components, since a GPU
shortens dense encoding and reranking but not Lucene; what
we expect to survive is the ordering, with BM25 the cheapest
component per query and the cross-encoder the most ex-
