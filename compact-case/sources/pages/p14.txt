B. WHEN DOES FUSION HELP?
The quadrant decomposition (Table 7) makes the mechanism
explicit. Fusion helps when the two retrievers are comple-
mentary and comparably reliable on a query: in Both-Win
the two rankings can promote different relevant documents,
although Table 7 shows that the reordering runs both ways
there and nearly cancels, and in Both-Fail fusion can only
pull a relevant document up from below the cutoff. Fusion
hurts when one retriever is decisively better and the other
contributes noise: in Dense-Only and Sparse-Only, fusion
dilutes the strong signal and demotes documents the better
retriever had already surfaced, and a dense-heavy weight
sacrifices the Sparse-Only minority almost entirely (87–95%
hurt). Because a stronger dense encoder widens its lead on
the queries it already wins without shrinking the off-diagonal
share, the tax does not decline along the ladder. The value
of fusion on a corpus is thus governed by the balance and
complementarity of its two retrievers on that corpus’s query
distribution, not by the absolute quality of either.
C. CORPUS PATTERNS IN BRIEF
SciFact (precise scientific terminology) has the cleanest pro-
file: 76% Both-Win and the lowest tax after Quora. FiQA is
among the hardest, with 30% Both-Fail and a 23% Dense-
Only share where only semantics help. NFCorpus carries the
highest tax outside the 50-query TREC-COVID set (39%)
because heavy synonymy gives both retrievers partially rel-
evant documents that fusion frequently reorders. ArguAna
is dense-dominated (20% Dense-Only) and pays a 30% tax
even though, with the standard BM25, fusion matches dense-
only in aggregate. SciDocs resembles a harder SciFact, with a
third of its queries paying the tax. TREC-COVID (50 queries,
100% Both-Win, balanced retrievers) is the regime in which
rank fusion wins and fusion helps most queries, yet it still
pays the highest oracle tax because when both retrievers rank
a relevant document first, fusion can only displace it. Quora
(duplicate questions) is dense-dominated and accurate, so
fusion has the least to add and incurs the lowest tax (13%),
yet even there it demotes relevant documents on one query in
eight.
