B. RETRIEVAL METHODS
Sparse. BM25 is the Anserini implementation [11] run
through Pyserini [12] (version 1.6, Java 21) over the offi-
cial prebuilt BEIR Lucene indexes [14], in the multifield
configuration (title and body indexed as separate fields)
with Anserini’s default parameters k1 = 0.9, b = 0.4.
Section V-A shows that these runs reproduce the pub-
lished Anserini nDCG@10 on all seven corpora to four
decimals. The single-field (“flat”) Anserini index and the
Python rank_bm25 implementation [77] serve only as an
implementation-sensitivity check (Supplementary Table S1).
Dense. We use five embedding models spanning 22M–
596M parameters and three model generations (2021,
2023, 2025): all-MiniLM-L6-v2 [78] (22M, 384-d), BGE-
small/base/large-en-v1.5
[79]
(33M/110M/335M),
and
Qwen3-Embedding-0.6B [80] (596M, 1024-d, released
2025). All corpora are searched with exact inner product
(FAISS [81]). Detailed per-query analysis uses BGE-small;
the BGE ladder is the controlled same-family scale-up,
MiniLM the cross-family check, and Qwen3 the newer-
generation check. Queries are encoded prefix-free with
the BGE models throughout; Supplementary Table S8 re-
encodes the queries with the model-card instruction and
shows that the tax is insensitive to the choice: the instruction
raises dense nDCG@10 by at most 0.026 on five corpora
(under 0.02 in all but one model–corpus pair), by 0.09–0.11
on TREC-COVID, and by 0.06 for BGE-base and BGE-large
on Quora, and it moves the interpolation tax by at most three
points on the five corpora other than TREC-COVID and
Quora, where it removes the five-point penalty that prefix-
free encoding imposes on BGE-base and BGE-large. Qwen3-
Embedding is instruction-tuned and is used with the query
instruction prescribed by its model card, with documents
truncated at 512 tokens like the other encoders.
Learned sparse. SPLADE [15] (the CoCondenser-
EnsembleDistil checkpoint [16]) replaces BM25 as the
sparse side in Section V-F, scored by exact sparse dot prod-
uct.
Fusion. RRF with the literature default k = 60; normal-
ized score interpolation, sf = α ˜sd + (1 −α) ˜ss with min–
max normalization over each retriever’s top-100 and dense
weight α; and CombMNZ [17]. The interpolation weight
Chauhan and Agarwal: The Fusion Tax: A Diagnostic Analysis of When Hybrid Retrieval Hurts
is held fixed at α = 0.7 for every encoder and corpus in
every table unless stated otherwise, so no encoder or corpus
is tuned within this study; Section V-B selects α under
two held-out protocols and reports the tax at the selected
weights. All retrievers return the top-100 documents per
query, and scores and ranks are stored per query. Exact fused-
score ties, frequent under RRF because its contributions take
finitely many values, are broken by document identifier in
the trec_eval convention, so fused rankings and the candidate
lists of Section IV-C are deterministic.
C. RERANKING AND EFFICIENCY MEASUREMENTS
To measure how much of the tax a second stage recovers, we
rerank with the cross-encoder BGE-reranker-v2-m3 [82]. For
each query, the top-30 candidates of BM25, dense, RRF, and
interpolation are scored once as a union, and each arm is then
re-sorted by the cross-encoder score, so the arms differ only
in their candidate source and share an identical reranking
budget (all four reranked arms are listed in Supplementary
Table S10). Quora is subsampled to 1,000 queries (seed 42)
for cost. The reranker runs in 16-bit floating point; we veri-
fied on samples that its rankings agree with 32-bit scoring to
within 0.004 nDCG@10. Efficiency is measured on a single
consumer workstation (Apple M5, 16 GB unified memory, no
discrete GPU): retrieval and fusion latencies on its CPU cores
(batch size one, warm caches; median and 95th percentile
over 200 queries), and reranker throughput on its integrated
GPU; index sizes are measured on disk (Lucene) or as the
embedding matrix (dense).
D. EVALUATION METRICS AND STATISTICS
nDCG@10, the standard BEIR cutoff, is the primary metric
throughout, computed with pytrec_eval [83]; recall@10
is reported where coverage matters. Pairwise comparisons
use Wilcoxon signed-rank tests with Bonferroni correction
and paired Cohen’s d; 95% confidence intervals use 1,000
bootstrap resamples of queries; risk-sensitive comparisons
against the deployable baseline use URisk/TRisk [57], [58];
variance decomposition uses one-way ANOVA η2.
E. QUERY FEATURES FOR PREDICTABILITY
We extract 17 features per query in three groups. Query-
intrinsic (6): query length, IDF mean/max/std, entity density,
distance to the corpus centroid. Retrieval score-based (5):
BM25 and dense top-1 minus top-2 margins, mean and
standard deviation of the dense top-10 similarities, top-10
Jaccard overlap between the two rankings. QPP-inspired
(6): NQC and WIG [46], [47] for each retriever, their ratio,
and a margin-based score-ratio weight. A gradient-boosted
tree ensemble (GBT; 200 trees, depth 3) predicts fusion
failure, defined as τ(q) > 0.01 to exclude near-ties, and is
evaluated by held-out ROC-AUC both within a corpus (5-
fold) and across corpora (leave-one-corpus-out).
